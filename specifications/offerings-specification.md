# Offerings Specification

Defines the three offering types on Lumaya — **events**, **retreats**, **trainings** — and the editorial line on what belongs and what does not. Grounded in Lumaya's brand essence.

## 1. Brand foundation

Every editorial decision below derives from these. Where there's tension, the brand essence wins.

### Mission
> Lumaya connects people with conscious events that nurture growth, deepen connection, and spark positive change through shared, intentional experiences.

### Vision
> We envision a world where participating in transformative practices is easily accessible for everyone, both locally and while travelling — fostering a more connected, compassionate, and sustainable world.

### Brand values

| Value | What it means for offerings |
|---|---|
| **Personal Growth & Transformation** | Every offering should encourage self-exploration and continuous growth — mental, physical, or spiritual. |
| **Community & Connection** | Offerings should foster genuine human connection and co-creation, not isolated consumption. |
| **Ecological Responsibility** | We favour offerings that practice sustainability, nature immersion, and care for the planet. |
| **Transparent Quality** | Listings live alongside public, uneditable reviews. Trust is built openly, not curated away. |
| **Inclusivity & Welcoming Tone** | Beginners and seasoned practitioners both belong. No elitism, no gatekeeping. |
| **Modern & Grounded Aesthetic** | Spiritual *and* credible. Clean, minimalist, culturally sophisticated — never hippie kitsch or esoteric extreme. |

### Brand manifesto (excerpt)
> We are Lumaya — curators of transformation. […] Our platform is a bridge — linking you to ancient wisdom and modern wellness, to city studios and forest retreats, to yourself, each other, and the earth.

The phrase **"ancient wisdom *and* modern wellness"** is the editorial centre of gravity. Either side without the other tips us off-brand: pure ancient wisdom risks looking esoteric and inaccessible; pure modern wellness risks being commodity fitness.

## 2. The three offering types

### 2.1 Events

**Definition.** A single occurrence or short-format gathering, locally attended, with low commitment.

| Property | Typical event |
|---|---|
| Duration | A few hours to a single day |
| Location | One venue, near where the user lives or travels |
| Commitment | Drop-in / single ticket |
| Format | Group session, open or semi-open enrolment |
| Outcome | A felt experience — practice, connection, presence |
| Certification | None |
| Examples | Ecstatic dance night · sound bath · cacao ceremony · breathwork class · women's circle · public lecture · weekend yoga workshop |

**User intent.** *"What's on tonight / this weekend near me?"* — discovery is impulsive and proximity-driven.

### 2.2 Retreats

**Definition.** A multi-day, destination-anchored immersion. The user travels to it and stays.

| Property | Typical retreat |
|---|---|
| Duration | 2 days to 3 weeks (most: 5–9 days) |
| Location | Destination — often abroad, often nature-based |
| Commitment | Booked cohort with fixed start/end |
| Format | All-inclusive (accommodation, meals, programme) |
| Outcome | A sustained reset — deep practice, integration, lifestyle pause |
| Certification | None (or optional) |
| Examples | 7-day yoga retreat in Bali · 10-day silent Vipassana · ayahuasca retreat in Costa Rica · breathwork retreat in Portugal · women's circle retreat |

**User intent.** *"Where can I disappear to this season?"* — aspirational, planned, often started before exact dates are fixed.

### 2.3 Trainings

**Definition.** A certifying or skill-building course that produces a practitioner, facilitator, or teacher.

| Property | Typical training |
|---|---|
| Duration | Several weeks to many months (intensive or modular) |
| Location | Single site (intensive) or distributed (modular / online) |
| Commitment | Cohort-based, formal enrolment, significant fee |
| Format | Curriculum-led, often with mentorship and assessment |
| Outcome | A formal qualification or certification |
| Certification | Yes — Yoga Alliance, IAYT, school-issued, etc. |
| Examples | 200-hour YTT · Somatic Experiencing training · breathwork facilitator certification · sound healing certification · life coach certification |

**User intent.** *"Which cohort can I commit to next year?"* — planned, financially significant, life-restructuring.

### 2.4 Quick-decision matrix

When a listing is ambiguous, classify by the dominant property:

| Question | If yes → |
|---|---|
| Issues a certification or formal qualification? | **Training** |
| Multi-day, includes accommodation, single cohort? | **Retreat** |
| Single session or short workshop, drop-in attendance? | **Event** |

A weekend workshop without certification is an **event**, not a training. A 2-day yoga workshop with accommodation in a retreat centre is a **retreat**, not an event. A 3-month online course that issues a certificate is a **training**, even if individual modules feel event-like.

## 3. What we want

An offering belongs on Lumaya if it satisfies **most** of these:

1. **Transformative intent.** The offering exists to support growth, healing, presence, or connection — not just entertainment or consumption.
2. **Conscious frame.** Rooted in established traditions (yoga, meditation, breathwork, somatic work, dance, ceremony, plant medicine, healing modalities) or modern adaptations of them.
3. **Genuine practice.** Real teachers, real practices, accountable facilitators. Quality is verifiable through participation and reviews.
4. **Accessible.** Welcoming to beginners as well as experienced practitioners. No prerequisites beyond curiosity and basic respect for the practice.
5. **Community-oriented.** Group format, shared experience, space for connection. Even solo-feeling practices (silent meditation) happen *together*.
6. **Aesthetic fit.** Modern, grounded presentation. The listing wouldn't look out of place next to a thoughtful magazine feature or a contemporary studio brand.
7. **Lawful & safe.** Operates within local law and within reasonable safety standards for the practice (e.g., trained facilitators, integration support for plant medicine, informed consent).

### Concrete examples we want

- Yoga (all styles), meditation (all traditions), breathwork
- Ecstatic dance, conscious movement, 5 Rhythms, Biodanza
- Sound baths, soundjourneys, kirtan, drum circles
- Cacao ceremonies, sweat lodges, fire ceremonies, traditional plant-medicine work with experienced facilitators
- Somatic therapy, trauma-informed work, IFS, family constellations
- Authentic relating, NVC, women's/men's/mixed circles
- Tantra workshops (clearly framed)
- Ayurveda, traditional healing modalities
- Nature immersion, forest bathing, hiking-with-practice retreats
- Teacher trainings, facilitator certifications, coaching certifications from recognised lineages

## 4. What we don't want

The line is between **established practices and traditions** (in) and **pseudoscientific or harmful claims** (out). The brand value *Modern & Grounded Aesthetic* exists exactly to keep us from drifting into the second category.

### 4.1 Pseudo-science and bogus health claims (hard no)

These dilute trust, alienate the modern audience, and can cause real harm. Reject regardless of how popular they are in adjacent spaces.

- **Lichtnahrung / Breatharianism** — claims that humans can live without food
- **Quantenheilung / Quantum healing / Quantum energetics / "Quantenwelle"** — misuse of quantum-physics vocabulary to make medical claims
- **Bioresonance / Scalar energy / Orgone devices** — pseudo-scientific medical devices and "energy frequency" cures
- **"Frequency" / "vibration" healing that claims medical outcomes** — distinct from sound bath as relaxation/practice; the line is the *claim*
- **Detox cures making medical claims** — colon cleanses, parasite cleanses, "heavy metal detoxes" framed as cures
- **Anti-vaccine workshops, "natural immunity" courses, vaccine-detox protocols**
- **MMS / chlorine dioxide, "miracle" supplement schemes**
- **Cancer-cure or chronic-illness-cure claims** of any kind
- **Conspiracy-spirituality content** (QAnon-adjacent, "great awakening", flat-earth-spiritual hybrids)
- **Multi-level marketing dressed as training** (essential-oil MLMs, "coach certifications" that are funnels into pyramid recruiting)

### 4.2 Off-brand for "conscious events"

Categories that aren't pseudoscience but fall outside Lumaya's frame. Already removed from the taxonomy (May 2026):

- **Recreational / social dance** — bellydance, latin-dance, zouk, balfolk, folk-dance, salsa, ballroom (these are wonderful but they're nightlife / hobby, not conscious-movement)
- **Generic music events** — concerts, music lessons, jam sessions, instrument-specific events without a contemplative frame
- **Recreational fitness** — bootcamps, CrossFit, weight-loss programmes, commercial spa packages
- **Commerce / business events** — entrepreneurship workshops, sales trainings, networking-only events
- **Generic dating / singles events** — hookup or matchmaking events; *conscious dating circles* with explicit transformative framing are a different question (see §5)
- **Generic talks formats** — webinars, podcasts, congresses, book launches, theatre, film screenings (unless explicitly contemplative or experiential)

### 4.3 Edge of acceptable — host with care

Listed but flagged `sensitive: true` and gated through editorial review:

- **Plant medicine** (ayahuasca, psilocybin, iboga, MDMA-assisted, etc.) — only with experienced facilitators, informed consent, integration support, and lawful operation in jurisdiction
- **Tantra / conscious sexuality / shibari** — only with explicit consent culture, professional facilitation, no coercive lineages
- **Shamanism** — only from facilitators with traceable lineage and ethical practice; reject neo-shamanic appropriation tourism
- **Channeling, mediumship, akashic records, card readings** — accepted as practices; reject when they make medical or financial-prediction claims
- **Intensive group-process modalities** (Landmark-style, breathwork intensives) — reject when they show cult markers (love-bombing, isolation, escalating financial pressure)

### 4.4 Hard prohibitions (legal / safety)

- Anything illegal in the host jurisdiction
- Events promoting harm to self or others
- Hate, discriminatory, or exclusionary content (note: women-only / men-only spaces are inclusion-of-identity, not exclusion-of-other)
- Unaccompanied minors at adult-themed events
- Anything that violates the platform's content policy or Terms of Service

## 5. The gray zone

Some offerings live between "in" and "out". Default test: **does it meet the *transformative intent* and *conscious frame* tests?** When unsure, ask:

| Borderline case | Accept if … | Reject if … |
|---|---|---|
| Cooking workshop | Framed as conscious nutrition, ayurvedic cooking, ceremonial food, fermentation/foraging | Generic recipe class without contemplative or healing frame |
| Hiking retreat | Includes practice (meditation, forest bathing, embodiment) | Pure adventure tourism / mountaineering |
| Surf retreat | Surf + yoga / breathwork / community frame | Pure sport |
| Conscious dating event | Explicit consent culture, relating skills, shadow-work component | Speed dating / pickup-style |
| Business / leadership training | Conscious leadership, regenerative business, mindful entrepreneurship | Hustle culture, sales tactics, MLM |
| "Quantum" anything | Used metaphorically with no health claims and within an established practice | Used to make medical claims or sell devices |
| Ice bath / cold exposure | Wim Hof method, breathwork-integrated, supervised | Endurance challenge / fitness flex without context |

## 6. Decision framework

When evaluating a new listing or category proposal, run through this in order:

1. **Type check** — is this an event, retreat, or training per §2? If it doesn't fit any cleanly, it probably doesn't belong.
2. **Want check** — does it satisfy most of §3 (transformative intent, conscious frame, accessibility, community)?
3. **Don't-want check** — does it trip any §4.1 or §4.4 trip-wire? Hard reject.
4. **Off-brand check** — does it fall under §4.2? Reject for the platform's identity, not for being bad.
5. **Sensitivity check** — does it need a `sensitive: true` flag and editorial review per §4.3?
6. **Gray zone** — apply §5; default to *transformative intent + conscious frame*.
7. **Aesthetic check** — could the listing sit alongside the rest of Lumaya without dragging the brand toward "hippie esoteric" or "commodity fitness"? If not, decline or request a reframed listing.

If steps 1–4 pass and 5–7 are clean, accept. If 5 flags it, route to editorial. If anything before that fails, decline with the specific reason — host honesty over vague rejection.

## 7. One-line summary

**Lumaya hosts established practices for personal growth and connection, presented with modern credibility — and declines pseudo-scientific health claims, recreational substitutes, and esoteric extremes.**
